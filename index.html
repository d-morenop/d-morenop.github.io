About

I am a theoretical physicist working on ice-sheet modelling, with a particular interest in the dynamics and stability of the Antarctic ice sheet.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Laboratoire de Glaciologie of the Université libre de Bruxelles (ULB). My research combines physical descriptions, numerical methods, and stochastic processes to investigate the future evolution of West Antarctica, with a strong focus on Thwaites Glacier.

My interests include numerical modelling, grounding-line dynamics, ice-sheet stability, and sea-level projections.

Research

I am interested in the physical processes that dictate the evolution of the Earth System, with a focus on the cryosphere. My work is strongly collaborative, and I am developing the following models:

Nix

A two-dimensional thermomechanical ice sheet model with a Python interface. Nix can be easily forced with any external boundary conditions. It has been benchmarked for standard test problems, showing versatility from regular laptops (lightweight memory allocation) to high-performance computing (multi-threading capabilities).

Publication: Geoscientific Model Development (2025)

Ice thermodynamics

A model to benchmark ice-sheet thermal solvers. Anaytical solutions of the heat equation considering advection, diffusion and strain dissipation.

Publication: The Cryosphere (2024)

Kori-ULB

Higher-order velocity solver: Depth-Integrated Viscosity Approximation (DIVA).

Time-step optimization for over-implicit to fully explicit schemes.

Stochastic climate variability module.

Implementation of a grain-size based tensile stress model.

Publication: Publication information to be added

Projects

I am also participating in two model intercomparison projects:

ISMIP7

Comparing the contributions of Antarctica and Greenland to future sea-level rise of various models over the next coming centuries.

CalvingMIP

An EU-funded world-wide model intercomparison project on ice damage and calving, comparing the novel routines to standard approaches/calving laws, leading to recommendations for improved calving laws in ice sheet models.
